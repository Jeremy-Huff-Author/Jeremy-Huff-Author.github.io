The market was a menagerie of humanity: porters, drovers, and drivers, their carts pulled by goat or burbling boilers; ale drapers, ale drinkers; alley witches with their alley cats, selling cure-alls and curses;  proselytizers and prostitutes, one cursing vice the other peddling it; the occasional interloping uptower dilettante. There were dealers of mohair jackets, cotcloth gloves, kid caps, and tanned boots; vendors of victuals, the pale outdome vegetation, slick slabs of chevon, butchered pig-rat, sausages of unquestioned meat. Finally, and most profusely, was the dirty press of the market-goers and the quick flitting steps of their urchins darting through and between it all.
