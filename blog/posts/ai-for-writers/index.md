I’d like to say a word about AI, since everyone else seems to be.

I would like to focus on my own usage of AI in writing, and try to avoid, if I can, characterizing or proscribe other people’s usage. 

As a software developer, I have a lot of experience with AI, both as a tool for personal productivity, and as a component in application design. There is a lot of hype around it, and in areas it meets and exceeds the hype. In far more areas it fails to deliver on our hopes for it, or the promises of the companies selling it to us.

Now, with regards to writing, I have some very strict personal guidelines for the role that AI can/should take in my own writing journey. One taxonomy of AI usage in writing that I have found particularly helpful, is the distinction between AI Generated, and AI Assisted.

What is the difference? Let me take a crack at defining the terms.

## Terms

AI Generated: A work is AI Generated when any part of the prose that appears on the page was created by an Large Language Model (LLM). 

That is usually as far as the definition goes, but for me I would go a little farther, and say that I would also consider a story, in which the plot outline was created by an LLM, to be AI Generated.

AI Assisted: It is tempting to define this relative and opposite AI Generated, and just say that all other uses of AI in writing are considered AI Assisted. And that might work, but in an attempt to be a little more useful, I will outline some of those potential uses:



- Research 
- Concept Art Creation
- Idea Generation
- Editorial Feedback: Developmental, Line, Copy, or Proof. I designate this as “feedback” only, because I would consider any prose that was rewritten by an LLM to be AI Generated, and not AI Assisted.
- Alpha/Beta Reader Style Feedback


There are many more things, I am sure, but I just wanted to name a few for the purposes of illustration.

## So How do I Use AI?

Well, I want to start by saying that I will never use AI Generation in my writing. I have absolutely no interest in something else writing for me. I would also never higher a ghost writer. I want to write books, because I want to write books. 

I have been reading words written by others my whole life. I started writing so that I can contribute my own words. Using AI to create prose for me would just be more of the same—me reading words I did not create.

Now, that’s out of the way.

The following are areas where I find AI usage, at the very least, does not inhibit my ability to be the creative force behind my writing.

### Research

I have used AI extensively for gathering information and attempting to ground my speculation in fact. Anything that I might have googled for in the past, I typically will take to a chat bot now. The ability to generate quick and mostly accurate responses has sped my research up tremendously.

Now, this comes with all the typical caveats. 

You must verify what the LLM tells you. Hallucinations happen far less now than they did even a year ago, but they do still happen.

That said, I think the same can be said for the random information that you might find through a google search. Verification of information was always the responsibility of the researcher, and that has not changed with the coming of AI.

### Concept Art Creation

I have used AI extensively to visualize elements of the speculative world I am creating. It has been a joy to see rendered things that have lived for so long in my head.

One creative hazard I have discovered here, is that I might be tempted to compromise my creative vision for a concept when I encounter an AI generated image that is close but not quite right. I might say to myself, “Sure, that looks pretty cool. It’s close enough.”

For me, I think it is important to have integrity in your vision, and ensure that you are not allowing the shortcomings of AI to dictate the creative direction of your work.

That said, once again, the same is true for working with human artists. 

And about human artists: I would far prefer to work with a human artist to create the images I use in concept planning, but I do not have the resources to do so.

When it comes to concept art creation, I see very little difference in using AI to generate concept art, and using Pinterest create a mood board images scraped off the internet. In both cases you are borrowing depictions you did not create to express an approximation of the vision you have for your writing.

### Editorial Feedback

This is most likely the most controversial usage of AI which I engage in.

As with concept art, I would prefer to engage a human editor for my work, but lack the resources to do so. In the future, if I have a human editor available, I will utilize them for editing over AI without question.

In the meantime, AI can assist in editing, with some important caveats.

The first being, it is not great at it. It is better at proofing and copy editing than it is at line or developmental edits.

In the case of line edits, it can be especially difficult to get AI to not attempt to destroy your authorial voice. If you take every suggestion that AI gives you, your writing would be come bland and lifeless.

But, with the proper guidance, and the discipline to put your creative vision first, it is possible for AI to draw your attention to problem areas in your writing. For me this has helped tremendously with speeding up the editorial processes.

Lastly, I find it essential to give the LLM specific instructions to list out areas that need editorial attention, but to not create rewrites of those sections. This empowers me to address the issues in my own authorial capacity.
