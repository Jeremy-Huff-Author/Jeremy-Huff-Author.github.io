I think I'd like to take a moment to describe the specific novel I am attempting to write. Learning how to talk about something you created is hard to do, and this will be good practice.

I am writing a speculative fiction book, set far off into a very extensively developed potential future. I am not exactly sure what separates fantasy from science fiction, but I feel like placing this story on some distant future point of our own timeline tilts things pretty firmly in the scifi direction.


**The Spark**

In 2005 I was on a road trip with my father and my brother. We were driving from Texas, where we lived, to Lake Charles Louisiana to work with some folks who were recovering from Hurricane Rita. On this trip I posed a question to the two of them: what would it take to justify a Steam Punk setting with hard science?

This resulted in a discussion that not only filled up the hours of that car trip, but many more hours in years to come. After returning home the conversation continued over the next few months and grew beyond just the three of us. It became the first experience I have had with communal world building.

Over the years since then I have written and expanded, and expanded again on many of the ideas that were explored by my family and friends. But one thing that will always be unique about the setting of Altura is that it was born out of a shared exploration of speculation.

My initial motivation for writing this book was to bring the world of Altura to others. I really wanted to create a vehicle for exploring that place, and to share it with others people who might find it as compelling as we all did so long ago.

That said, I really don't want any book that I write to take a back seat to the setting it exists in. I feel like that would be a fools errand and not worth my time spent writing it or other's time spent reading it.

So the challenge was to develop a story that deserved to exist in its own right, while allowing the planet of Altura to share shine out and capture the imaginations of others.
