Hello folks!

I think I’d like to take a moment to describe the specific circumstances that set me down this path to write a novel. It all started about 20 years ago on a road trip.

The Spark

In 2005 I was on a road trip with my father and my brother. We were driving from Texas, where we lived, to Lake Charles Louisiana to work with some folks who were recovering from Hurricane Rita. On this trip I posed a question to the two of them: what would it take to justify a Steam Punk setting with hard science?

This resulted in a discussion that not only filled up the hours of that car trip, but many more hours in years to come. After returning home the conversation continued over the next few months and grew beyond just the three of us. It became the first experience I have had with communal world building.

Over the years since then I have written and expanded, and expanded again on many of the ideas that were explored by my family and friends. But one thing that will always be unique about the setting of Altura is that it was born out of a shared exploration of speculation.

My initial motivation for writing this book was to bring the world of Altura to others. I really wanted to create a vehicle for exploring that place, and to share it with others people who might find it as compelling as we all did so long ago.

The World

In the future, I will take time to dive into the specific details of how the planet of Altura is the way it is, but for now I would like to paint it in with broad brush strokes.

Imagine a world where humanity survives above and below layers of sky—where the planet’s surface lies hidden beneath a dense, poisonous fog, while a golden-blue sea of breathable air floats high above. This strange division is the legacy of a failed terraforming project: the breathable air, lighter than the native gases, now relegated to the heights while the ground is lost to clinging mists. Scattered across the planet, immense towers thrust through the gloom, their summits anchoring floating cities—Aerlands—crafted from buoyant “coral” grown by cultivated native microbes. Here, industrail technologies reigns supreme: the sun’s wild electromagnetic storms have made electronic technology unreliable, so society has rebuilt itself on intricate clockwork, pressure, and steam.

Most people shelter in the domed cities at the base of each tower or live among the clouds in the floating Aerlands, but rumors persist of those who have adapted to the toxic depths—becoming something both human and other, haunting the shrouded wilds beneath the towers.

What next?

In the future, I’ll be writing some deep dives into the specific attempts that have been made to ground this fantastical arrangement in hard science: the exodus of humanity from our solar system and their search for a new home aboard a fleet of generation ships; the colonization effort that transformed an inhospitable land into a “world house” fit for human survival. I want to explore the orchestrated attack on the shielding that protected Altura from its violent sun, the collapse of the world house, and the cataclysmic event known as Skyfall.

I hope these things are as interesting to others as they have been for me over the years.
