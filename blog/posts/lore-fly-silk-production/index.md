Altura is a world divided. It is divided physically—the atmosphere of the planet has a stark divide between breathable oxegen above and toxic Nitros Oxide below—and it is divided socially. The impoverished masses of the planet crowd into domes at the base of ancient towers, sheltered from the suffocating air, but trapped by it at the same time. Above them, in magnificent floating cities called aerlands, the aristocrats lead very different lives—lives of luxury and freedom.

The divide between the peoples of Altura is stark, and invades every aspect of life. Including the clothes which are worn by each group. At the base of the towers rough spun cot cloth and mohair are the primary textiles used to clothe the domers. In the aerlands, the elite wear the finest fly silk clothing, an immediate distinction of class.



Here’s a detailed explanation of the Silk Fly and the production of Fly Silk:

## The Silk Fly: An Aerial Angler


The Silk Fly (Myia filamenta) is a native invertebrate species of Altura’s upper atmosphere, adapted exquisitely to its unique environment. Roughly the size of a marble, the Silk Fly achieves buoyancy by inflating a semi-flexible air bladder filled with oxygen harvested and stored via the native coral’s internal reservoirs. This ingenious adaptation allows the creature to drift effortlessly among the coral clusters that float in the upper layers of Altura’s native toxic atmosphere.

In nature, the Silk Fly produces an exceptionally fine protein filament — known as Fly Silk — to anchor itself securely to coral outcroppings. This silk serves both as a tether and as a stabilizing mechanism during the creature’s windborne foraging. The thread allows the Silk Fly to hover in place amid shifting air currents, optimizing its position for feeding or evading predators. The line is remarkably strong relative to its size, withstanding the variable forces of Altura’s upper winds without breakage. When the Silk Fly completes a drift or relocates, it either retracts its silk or severs the line, leaving the residual thread fused to the coral structure.

## Cultivation and Harvesting by the Flysilk Guild


Recognizing the immense value of Fly Silk, the people of Altura developed sophisticated methods to cultivate, manage, and harvest Myia filamenta colonies. Guilds formed around these practices and knowledge. Now these guilds operate habitat scaffolds designed to simulate natural coral formations, where Silk Flies can safely nest and weave their threads under monitored conditions.

### Cultivation:


The Guild’s cultivators manage the health of both the Silk Fly colonies and their coral anchors. Artificial wind manipulation is used to blow the Silk Flies from these scaffold, encouraging the flies to produce their silk lines during controlled drifts. These conditions mimic the natural currents that prompt the flies to spin their threads while tethering themselves.

### Harvesting:


The Guild distinguishes between two principal forms of Fly Silk, each requiring unique harvesting techniques:

Prime Line Silk (Fresh-Drift): Harvested while the Silk Fly is still tethered and actively spinning. This grade of silk is highly prized for its length, purity, and suppleness. Harvesters use fine instruments to collect the line mid-drift without breaking or tangling it. Any damage during this delicate process renders the thread unsuitable for high-end textile applications.

Coral-Fused Silk (Scrape-Silk): Obtained from the remains of severed silk lines that adhere to coral clusters. These threads are thicker, often brittle, and encrusted with coral dust or biofilm. Scrape-silk is collected using lime-oiled blades to prevent tearing during extraction.

The Guild enforces strict controlls surrounding Fly Silk production. Unauthorized harvesting is outlawed to prevent ecological harm and maintain the sustainability of the species and its coral habitat.

## Fly Silk in Textile Creation


Fly Silk is a cornerstone material in Altura’s textile economy, especially among the elite of the Aerlands.

Prime Line Silk is spun into luxurious fabrics renowned for their extraordinary softness, durability, and subtle sheen. It is used in gowns and evening wear, military dress uniforms, high-market garments, and other prestigious attire associated with the sky’s nobility.

Coral-Fused Silk finds application in utilitarian textiles — travel cloaks, utilitarian military dress, and industrial filters. Its clouded luster and rugged texture are worn by the merchants and military personal for practical garments that endure the harsh conditions of of travel in the skies.

Each thread of Fly Silk, whether destined for finery or function, represents a convergence of natural wonder, human ingenuity, and guild tradition — a symbol of Altura’s delicate balance between nature and civilization.
