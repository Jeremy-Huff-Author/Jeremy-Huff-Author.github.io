Until recently I have been stuck on my rewrite of the second chapter of this novel I am working on.



The chapter is from the perspective of my female protagonist. I had an open few paragraphs that I was very pleased with, and then… I could not write any more. I thought I would wait a couple of days for inspiration to hit, but I quickly realized that this was just an avoidance tactic.



I decided to sit down and figure out my issue was. Even though the chapter was outlined, and the plot beats which I needed to carry out were right in front of me. I had no idea how to continue. I could not imagine the very next moment for this character.



After long consideration I discovered a very simple reasons why I was stuck.



This was that I had, in my opening paragraphs described a sequence of actions that a person would typically perform in the morning. But the next plot beat was one that should occur significantly later in the day.



Because I am slow on the uptake, I kept trying to solve this problem by thinking through what my character was going to spend the intervening hours doing. That, was a horrible way to approach the issue. 



The much more obvious solution, which I am sure is apparent to anyone reading this, was to change the actions the character is engaging in to ones that would occur closer to the time of my next story beat. By changing the time of day, I was able to adjust the details of the introductory paragraphs, but still leave the heart of what I liked about those handful of sentences.



I have heard it described as “shoe leather”. The idea is that you start a seen as deep into the action as you can get away with. If a man needs to talk to someone at the counter, you don’t need to describe the sound of his shoes as he walks across the room. Just start with him standing at the counter.



I intuited that everything that I could explored for my character in those intervening hours was going to be boring shoe leather. I just needed to start closer to the action.



Hopefully this will be a lesson learned.
